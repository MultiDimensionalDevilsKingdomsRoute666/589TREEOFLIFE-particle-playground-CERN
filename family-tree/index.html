Family Tree of Collisions

The eight CMS collision events from the playground's dataset grow into one Tree of Life. Every glowing branch is one particle from those events. Its length is its momentum (pT), its lean is its direction (φ), and its height is how far forward it flew (η). Each family member holds one event (a few share, like family does).

Tap a fruit to meet the particle.

What are the branches?

Muons are heavy cousins of the electron that fly right through the whole detector, so CMS catches them in its outer chambers. Electrons stop in the crystal calorimeter in a bright little shower. Jets are sprays of particles born when a quark or gluon is knocked loose. Photons are particles of light. The dashed root is missing energy, a neutrino that slipped away unseen, which the detector only knows about by what's missing.

pT is momentum sideways to the beam in GeV. φ is the angle around the beam. η tells you how close to the beam line a particle flew, with 0 straight out the side and large values nearly along the beam.

Data: the playground's CMS event set (src/data/events.js), modeled on CMS Run 2010B open data, CERN Open Data Portal (CC0), DOI 10.7483/OPENDATA.CMS.A342.9982. The event set is a simplified educational reproduction of that data, not raw detector files.
